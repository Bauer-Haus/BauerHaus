Nexa Privacy Policy

Effective October 10, 2026 · Updated October 13, 2026 (update checks in the version downloaded from bauerhaus.io, and turning them off)

We have no way to read or save your data. Nexa has no servers of ours and no accounts, so nothing you send ever passes through us. Your messages, files and contacts are encrypted with keys that exist only on your Mac and the Macs of the people you talk to. We can't read them, store them, recover them or hand them over to anyone, because we never have them.

In short

  • Nexa collects no data. No accounts, no analytics, no tracking, no crash reporting of our own.
  • We never receive your messages, files, contacts, display name, IP address or anything about how you use the app.
  • Your messages leave your Mac only encrypted, through public relays run by others, which can't read them either.
  • The only thing we ever see is an email you choose to send us, such as a report.

This policy covers the Nexa Messenger app for macOS, made by BauerHaus LLC (“we”). It describes exactly what the app does with information, so you can check it against how the app behaves.

What we collect

Nothing. Nexa has no sign-up, no user accounts and no servers run by us. It contains no analytics, advertising, tracking or crash-reporting code, and it never sends us your messages, contacts, display name, IP address or usage information. We can't identify who uses Nexa.

The only time we receive anything is when you choose to email us, for example by sending a report (see Reports). If you have opted in to share crash data with developers in macOS, Apple may pass anonymous crash reports to us under Apple's own privacy policy.

What stays on your Mac

Your chats, contacts, files and settings are stored only on your Mac, inside Nexa's own sandboxed folder. The message database is encrypted with a key kept in your Mac's Keychain, and you can add a passphrase and Touch ID lock. Move to Backup (Settings › Backup) saves it to an encrypted file you keep, then erases all of it from the Mac.

What leaves your Mac, and who can see it

To deliver messages without a central server, Nexa uses public infrastructure run by independent operators. None of them are run by us, and none of them can read your messages.

Nostr relays (messages)

Each message is end-to-end encrypted on your Mac and sent to public Nostr relays, which hold it until your contact's Mac (or, in a group, each member's Mac) picks it up. A relay sees only encrypted data of a padded size, the time it was sent, and a one-time address that changes with every message. It can't see your name (there are no accounts), who you are talking to, or what you said. In Standard mode it can see your IP address (see below). Nexa asks relays to delete messages after 14 days by default (you can change this), but relays are run by others and may keep the encrypted data longer. You can see and change which relays are used in Settings › Relays, including who operates each one. Group messages go to the relays of the person who made the group, and look exactly like private messages to the relay.

File servers (attachments)

Files you send are encrypted on your Mac and uploaded in pieces to public Blossom file servers, then downloaded by your contact. The servers see only encrypted pieces of a padded size. Nexa asks the servers to delete them once they are delivered. In a group, a file stays on the servers until it expires, because other members may not have downloaded it yet; the sender's Nexa then asks for it to be deleted.

Your IP address

In Maximum privacy mode, Nexa connects through the Tor network, and relays and file servers don't see your IP address. In Standard mode, Nexa connects directly, and each relay or file server you use can see your IP address, as any website you visit can.

Tor bridges

In Maximum privacy mode, Nexa normally reaches Tor through Tor's public relays. If your network blocks them, Nexa switches to a bridge by itself, and goes back to the public relays when you join another network. If using Tor could put you in danger, you can tell Nexa to always use a bridge (it asks when you first choose Maximum privacy), so it never contacts Tor's public relays, which your network could see. You can change this, or turn bridges off, in Settings › Network › Bridges. With Snowflake, a content delivery network passes one request from Nexa to the Tor Project's Snowflake service, which matches you with a volunteer's proxy; that proxy and the public STUN servers used to set up the connection see your IP address. With obfs4 or bridges you enter yourself, the bridge sees your IP address. None of them can see what you send, who you talk to, or that you are using Nexa: they only carry Tor's encrypted traffic.

DNS (Standard mode)

In Standard mode, Nexa looks up relay addresses with encrypted DNS, through Cloudflare by default, Quad9, or a provider you enter, under that provider's privacy policy. You can turn this off in Settings › Network to use your Mac's normal DNS. In Maximum privacy mode, lookups happen inside Tor.

Updates (the version downloaded from bauerhaus.io)

The copy of Nexa you download from bauerhaus.io checks bauerhaus.io for new versions, using the open-source Sparkle updater. It asks before it starts checking automatically, and you can turn automatic checks on or off at any time in Settings › Privacy › Updates (from version 1.0.1); with them off, it checks only when you choose Check Now or Check for Updates…. A check is an ordinary request for the update list. Like any app's request, it names the app and its version (for example "Nexa/1.0 Sparkle/2.9"); it sends nothing about you, your chats or your Mac. bauerhaus.io is hosted by GitHub Pages, so GitHub sees the request's IP address under its own privacy policy; we don't receive these requests or any logs of them. This request goes directly to bauerhaus.io, not through Tor, so while Nexa uses Tor it only checks when you choose Check for Updates…. The App Store version gets its updates from the App Store instead.

Your contacts

Your contacts receive what you send them, plus the display name you set in Settings, if any. Nobody else does: there is no directory, and nobody can find or message you without an invite link you share.

Group chats

A group has up to 10 people. You only join a group if you accept an invitation from someone you already chat with. Everyone in a group sees the list of members with their display names, and receives everything posted to the group, including your messages and files. The person who made the group decides who is in it and can remove people; anyone can leave. Group messages are end-to-end encrypted with keys only the members have, and we never see who is in a group or what is said in it.

Camera

Nexa uses the camera only when you choose to scan an invite or security-code QR code. Images are processed on your Mac and are never saved or sent anywhere.

Reports

If you report someone with Report…, Nexa writes an email draft to us in your own mail app, containing only the messages you choose to include and anything you type. Nothing is sent unless you send it. We use reports only to respond to you and to keep people safe, keep them only as long as needed for that, and never sell or share them, unless the law requires it.

Backups

Backups are files you save where you choose, encrypted with a recovery code that only you see. We never receive your backups or your code, and can't open or recover them.

Children

Nexa is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.

Your rights

Because we hold no personal data about you, there is nothing for us to access, correct, export or delete. All of your data is on your Mac and under your control. If you emailed us and want that email deleted, ask and we will delete it.

Changes

If this policy changes, we will update it on this page and change the effective date above. If a change affects what leaves your Mac, we will say so in the app's release notes.
